GAP-3  OPEN - a public-domain English of the freedom-of-the-will prize essay.
       He has the German (file 55) and no English. Four hunts named in the
       catalogue (passes 36, 37, 41, 45; twenty queries in all) produced nothing
       public-domain, and pass 45 proved the machinery with a positive control
       (476 items under his name), so the zero is the Archive and not the search.
       A query that adds a bare year to a quoted phrase ("On the freedom of the
       will" Schopenhauer 1864) returns nothing, because the year is not in the
       string searched - so years belong in date clauses, and date clauses are here
       untrusted (rule 47). What DOES exist at the Internet Archive under his name with
       "freedom" in the title is three modern printings, in copyright for our
       purposes: "Essay on the freedom of the will" (1960), "On the freedom of
       the will" (1985), "Prize essay on the freedom of the will" (1999). The
       E. F. J. Rowe translations of the 1860s, which would be the public-domain
       candidates, returned no Internet Archive title record at all this hour,
       and GutenDex was unreachable: NO DATA, not "does not exist".

GAP-10 OPEN - the LAST German state of "Ueber den Willen in der Natur".
       The estate holds the 1836 first edition (file 58) and the 1854 second
       (file 59, whose preface says as much: "die zweite, nachbessernde Hand ...
       nach 19 Jahren"). But Hillebrand's English says it translates the FOURTH
       edition, published by Frauenstaedt - the edition of 1873 - and that printing
       is not held here in German at all. Measured, the English is 1.45 times the
       1854 text, which is revision, not translation. So for the final form of this
       essay the collection's only continuous witness is a translation, and its
       wording is Hillebrand's, not his.
       HUNT 2026-10-26 (pass 51): the 1873/1874 Frauenstaedt volumes are on archive.org
       with a text layer, but it is garbled Fraktur OCR (see GAP-9), so the fourth
       edition's German wording remains NO DATA. Note that the readable Band 1 of 1877
       (ia_11173023) argues about this essay in the editor's preface: 'die lateinische
       Bearbeitung der Farbenlehre in die Gesammtausgabe aufnehmen zu muessen' - evidence
       about the edition's make-up, from the editor, not from his pen.

GAP-9  OPEN - the author's NOTES are missing from the Zeno transcriptions.
       File 01 (WWV I) and file 03 (the Fourfold Root) each carry a footnote
       SECTION that is an empty stub: the marker is there and four or five words
       follow it. Zeno put the notes of the book-length works somewhere the
       transcription did not follow. The consequence is measured, not guessed: the
       English of the same works prints them (file 08 has a 4,170-word FOOTNOTES
       block; file 11 carries 322 footnote markers), so a German search for
       something Schopenhauer said only in a note comes back empty, and the estate
       holds the English better than the German exactly where the German ought to
       be the witness. Until a copy with the notes is filed, treat files 01 and 03
       as the treatise WITHOUT its notes.
