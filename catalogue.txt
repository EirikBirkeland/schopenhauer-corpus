HOW A RECORD READS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  title        German title first, English title under "EN:"
  year         year of the German first edition; and, for a translation, the year
               of the translation printing that the file actually comes from
  edition      which edition/text the downloaded file was made from (stated in the
               file's own header too - every text file starts with a header block:
               tier, title, year, edition, translator, copyright, source, fetched)
  copyright    public domain = downloaded;  in copyright = catalogue only, the
               owner supplies his own copy (Hübscher, Payne, Cambridge)
  file         the UTF-8 plain text in OUT/schopenhauer/texts/
  source       URL read on the date given
  tier         1 PRIMARY | 2 SECONDARY | 3 GOSSIP

  Rules kept:  NO DATA = searched and not found (what was searched is named).
               UNVERIFIED = one source only.
               Where editions or translations disagree, both are shown.
               Nothing was estimated; every date above comes from a stated source.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW A PASSAGE IS CITED  (read this before quoting a number)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Page numbers are only worth their source. The [S. nnn] markers in files 01-03 come
  from Zeno.org's reproduction of the Zuercher Ausgabe - a scholarly READING edition in
  ten bands, with notes, not a full critical apparatus - so a Zeno page is a citation
  coordinate, not a print of the page. Markers in files 27, 30-52 were made only from
  running heads this collection could read, and are declared in each header; [p. n]
  markers in the secondary files are recovered from running heads by this collection and
  are editorial. Most Project Gutenberg translations have no pages at all: cite such a
  file by volume and chapter, never by page.
  Paragraph numbers are NOT universal, not even between two printings of the same book.
  The collected edition of 1877 (files 60-66) runs ONE AHEAD of the 1851 first edition
  (files 27, 30-53), verified at six points by matching opening sentences: 1851 sec. 61 /
  1877 sec. 62; 68/69; 103/104; 108/109; 142/143; 157/158. So "Parerga sec. 108" means
  nothing until the printing is named. Chapter numbers shift too: this collection cites
  Parerga's chapters by their words and pages, not by their numerals, because the numerals
  differ between the 1851 and 1877 arrangements and the OCR garbles them besides.
  The German, not the English, is the witness: a quotation is quoted from the German file
  and the translation is named alongside, since a translation may lose a word he chose
  (spot-checks 16, 20, 21) or soften it (spot-check 13).

            "Bestaetigungen" his philosophy has received from empirical science since its
            appearance - the wording now read in BOTH German copies, so the old
            UNVERIFIED mark is withdrawn. Beer (21, p. 33) renders it in English as

T3-01  CLAIM: "He threw the seamstress down the stairs; the story is a scuffle on
       a staircase."
       CIRCULATES: en.wikipedia, "Arthur Schopenhauer" (read 2026-10-25), which
       itself says "The details of the August 1821 incident are unknown. He
       claimed that he had just pushed her from his entrance after she had rudely
       refused to leave".
       HIS OWN RECORD: the court does not describe stairs at all. In the oath the
       plaintiff was put to (KG Instruktionssenat, 4 Oct 1824, file 24, [S. 1-2]):
       "daß sie am Abend des 12. August 1821 wirklich von dem Verkl. am Halse
       gefaßt und auf diese Art aus dem Zimmer hinausgeschleppt worden, daß ihr
       bei dieser Gelegenheit eine Warze abgerissen worden..." - he seized her by
       the neck and dragged her out of the room, and a wart was torn off.
       QUALIFIES IT: the insult for which he was already punished was his own:
       "daß er die Klägerin, eine Person des mittlern Bürgerstandes, altes Luder
       geschimpft" (KG Oberappellationssenat, 4 May 1827, file 25).
       QUALIFIES IT: the penalty and the money are documented, not legend: 20 rt,
       or 14 days' imprisonment in default, "wegen geringer Realinjurien" (file
       24, grounds, [S. 1]); the damages case (filed 6 Sept 1822) ran through the

       genius a target others cannot even see" - often printed as if it were the

       HIS OWN TEXT (file 02, WWV II, Ergänzungen zum dritten Buch, Kapitel 31 "Vom
       Genie", page marker S. 462 of the edition behind Zeno's text): "Das Talent
       gleicht dem Schützen, der ein Ziel trifft, welches die Uebrigen nicht
       erreichen können; das Genie dem, der eines trifft, bis zu welchem sie nicht
       ein Mal zu sehn vermögen: daher sie nur mittelbar, also spät, Kunde davon
       erhalten, und sogar diese nur auf Treu und Glauben annehmen."
       WHAT THAT SETTLES: (a) the line is his, in a simile of a SHOOTING-MATCH -
       talent hits a target the others cannot reach, genius one they cannot even

       were searched for it in pass 3 and neither has it (0 hits for "Talent" within
       120 characters of "trifft"); (c) the quotation almost always drops his last
       clause - genius is known only indirectly, therefore late, and only on trust -
       which is the actually pessimistic half of the remark.

T3-08  CLAIM: "He called Hegel a clumsy charlatan."
       CIRCULATES: en.wikipedia, l.cit. ("whom Schopenhauer described as a
       'clumsy charlatan'").
       HIS OWN TEXT: the word charlatan is his, "clumsy" is the writer's.
       German original, file 01:
         - Preface to the 2nd edition (Zürcher Bd 1, S. 16-17): he says he has
           long renounced the approval of contemporaries who worship "Scharlatanerei",
           and: "Es ist unmöglich, daß eine Zeitgenossenschaft, welche, zwanzig
           Jahre hindurch, einen Hegel, diesen geistigen Kaliban, als den größten
           der Philosophen ausgeschrien hat..." - Hegel is a "geistiger Kaliban",
           not a clumsy charlatan.
         - Anhang (Kritik der Kantischen Philosophie, Zürcher Bd 1, S. 514): the
           Hegelian philosophy "war der würdige Ausgangspunkt für den noch grobem
           Unsinn des plumpen und geistlosen Hegel" - HERE is "plumpen" (= clumsy,
           heavy), but joined to "geistlosen" (witless), not to "Charlatan".
       English, file 08 (preface as printed): "though, if it received its proper
       name, it would be called empty bombast and charlatanism"; file 09: "it
       reached its height, as every one knows, in Hegel, in whose hands it
       developed into pure charlatanism."
       RESULT: the substance is his and the words "plump" and "Scharlatanerei" are
       both his, but not in one phrase: what he wrote of Hegel was "plumpen und
       geistlosen" and "geistigen Kaliban". Wikipedia's "clumsy charlatan" is a
       tidy fusion of two different passages, one from S. 514, one from S. 16.

   BUT A PERCENTAGE MEANS NOTHING TILL ITS FLOOR AND ITS CEILING ARE MEASURED, and that is
   the whole of this spot-check. Same instrument, three calibrations, one paragraph and two
   chapters to hand:
     FLOOR   a chapter's opening against a window chosen for a DIFFERENT chapter:  20.3%
     SAME    the same text through two OCR layers, one chapter's opening:          73.3%
     CEILING a CLEAN transcription (file 05) against this scan of the same print:  57.6%
   Read those together and the conclusions are sharp. (i) 55-74% with a floor of 20% is what
   IDENTITY looks like when both layers are OCR - so the residual is the machines, exactly as
   pass 12 said, now with numbers attached. (ii) The clean transcription scores LOWER against
   this scan (57.6%) than OCR does against OCR (73.3%), because the Wikisource text is
   normalised to modern spelling while the scan keeps the print's shapes: an editor's page is
   further from a Fraktur sheet than a bad OCR of that sheet is. Anybody tempted to call a
   clean text a better comparator for measuring difference should read that twice. (iii) NO
   PERCENTAGE HERE PROVES IDENTITY, and therefore no percentage yet proves a VARIANT: claims
   about what the 1877 editors changed must wait for page images or a scholarly edition, and
   stay recorded as NO DATA in the meantime.

   "justo brevius" and in other words than his, mixing it with his own opinions. Everyone else
   who has since written on the matter in Germany has not attacked, refuted or condemned it, but
   "omnino de ea tacuerunt" - kept entirely silent, as is the way with trivial things not worth
   a dispute. And two sentences worth having in the vernacular: "non ea mea est humilitas, ut
   istorum silentium pro judicio habeam" - it is not my humility that takes their silence for a
   verdict; and "adversus merita silentium vituperatione saepe efficacius" - silence against
   merits is often more effective than blame (G-36).
   WHY THIS MATTERS TO THE SHELF: it puts a date and a name on the story of his neglect. In 1830
   he could name ONE supporter with his columns. That is worth more than the popular picture of
   a man unheard by all the world, and it comes from his own pen, in his own citation apparatus.
