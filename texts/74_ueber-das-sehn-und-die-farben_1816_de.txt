# note: ADDED IN PASS 32, and it closes GAP-2, the last item that register had held open since pass 2: the colour treatise is now in the collection in its FIRST EDITION. The book opens by saying what it is for - "eine neue Theorie der Farbe, die schon am Ausgangspunkte von allen bisherigen sich gaenzlich entfernt", written chiefly for readers of Goethe's Farbenlehre - and it names his debt and his quarrel at once: Goethe broke the Newtonian spell, but the cathedra and the writing-desks are still held by men whose whole life's honour depends on that doctrine not falling. WHY IT MATTERS TO THIS COLLECTION: the treatise is the standing witness for the Goethe quarrel, which files 73 and 75 report but which the earlier corpus could only cite from the letters and the 1854 preface; and it is the primary text behind the gossip that he was Goethe's disciple in optics, or Goethe's rival - check it against the fragments of the polemic in file 68 and against the Supplements chapter on colours in file 02 rather than trusting a biographer. The OCR is readable but is NOT a transcription: quote from it only after reading the line, and note that the volume has no page markers in the text layer, so cite by chapter (Einleitung, then the numbered sections) until another witness supplies pages. WHAT THE SECOND EDITION'S PREFACE RECORDS ABOUT THIS COPY (file 75, November 1854, and he is speaking of the book you are holding): he wrote the treatise in 1815; GOETHE KEPT THE MANUSCRIPT LONGER THAN HE EXPECTED, carrying it on his then journey along the Rhine, so that the final handling and the printing slipped and the little work only appeared at EASTER 1816; that neither physiologists nor physicists thought it worth considering and stayed with their textbooks; and that fifteen years later this neglect tempted a plagiarist to use it for his own profit. Read that sentence as his own account of the Goethe quarrel, dated, and not a disciple's story.
