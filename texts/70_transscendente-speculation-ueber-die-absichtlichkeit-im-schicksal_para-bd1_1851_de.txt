His essay on fate, and the one place he dares the risky thesis: that a life looks arranged, yet the arranging will is the man's own, working from a region outside individual consciousness - which is why he brings in animal magnetism and the somnambules here, as cases of cognition without the intellect. Note the sentence that gives the practical shape of it: since luck works against that purpose, misfortune and suffering are found woven into every life, though in very unequal measure, and only rarely in overplus - namely in tragic endings, where the will seems driven by force to turn from life, "und gleichsam durch den Kaiserschnitt zur Wiedergeburt gelangen sollte".
